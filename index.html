India Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) NAQI Reference Scale

The official Indian National Air Quality Index standard published by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC).

0 – 50
Good
Minimal impact. Ideal for all outdoor activities.
51 – 100
Satisfactory
Minor breathing discomfort to sensitive people.
101 – 200
Moderate
Breathing discomfort to people with lung disease, asthma and heart disease.
201 – 300
Poor
Breathing discomfort to most people on prolonged exposure.
301 – 400
Very Poor
Respiratory illness to people on prolonged exposure. Move workouts indoors.
401 – 500
Severe
Affects healthy people and seriously impacts vulnerable populations. Avoid outdoors.
Official Government Data Source Investigation Findings

CPCB & Data.gov.in Integration Review: CleanAir Window evaluated connecting directly to India's official government endpoints (the Open Government Data platform api.data.gov.in and CPCB's CAAQMS feed). Official data.gov.in APIs require private registered citizen/developer API keys, and CPCB's XML feed does not support client-side browser CORS headers or time-segmented daily slots. To guarantee reliable, secure client-side execution without exposing API credentials or breaking in browsers, clearly labelled simulated readings formatted strictly to CPCB NAQI specifications are provided for Pune, Mumbai, and Delhi.

General Guidance & Safety Disclaimer

Not Medical Advice: CleanAir Window provides general informational calculations derived from simulated mock data aligned with CPCB NAQI parameters. Readings do not reflect real-time live governmental broadcasts or certified local meteorological forecasts.

No Guarantee of Safe Breathing Conditions: Selecting the time slot with the lowest AQI indicates relative comparison within the day and does not guarantee safe atmospheric conditions. Individuals with respiratory conditions (such as asthma, COPD, bronchitis), cardiovascular disease, children, pregnant individuals, and older adults may experience adverse reactions even in "Satisfactory" or relatively cleaner windows. Always consult certified healthcare providers and official regional CPCB/SPCB bulletins before undertaking strenuous outdoor exercise.
